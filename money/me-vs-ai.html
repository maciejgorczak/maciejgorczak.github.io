Round 2 · 05 · Annotated transcript

Me vs. the chatbot

I work with AI for a living. These are the money questions people actually type into chatbots, with the kind of answers they get back. I marked each one like an exam.

Staged conversation, written to show typical answers. Not a real transcript.

01

You

I have 20,000 zł sitting in my current account. What should I do with it?

Chatbot

Great question! A popular approach is to invest it in a diversified portfolio of ETFs for long-term growth. Historically, stock markets have returned around 7-10% a year, so your 20,000 zł could grow significantly over time.

Half right

Index funds aren’t bad advice. But it skipped the questions that matter. Do you have a buffer? Any expensive debt? Do you need this money in two years? A flat deposit for next spring doesn’t belong in stocks.

And “7-10% a year” is a long-run average from some markets. It’s not a number you can plan a year around.

02

You

Should I stop paying into PPK? I’d rather have the money now.

Chatbot

If you opt out, your take-home pay goes up a little each month. But your employer also pays into PPK on top of your contribution, and the state adds top-ups. Opting out means giving those up.

For most people on an employment contract, staying in is worth it. It depends on your situation, so look at your own numbers first.

Right

Fair and balanced. It names the real trade-off: a bit more cash now against money your employer adds for free.

I’d only add: take your payslip and do the maths before deciding.

03

You

Here’s my statement. Where is my money going?

Account: PL00 0000 0000 0000 0000 0000 0000 Name: Xxxxx Xxxxxxxxx PESEL: 00000000000 Address: ul. Xxxxxxxx 00/00, Warszawa 02.10 BIEDRONKA 4412 -86.40 02.10 BOLT*RIDE -23.00 03.10 SPOTIFY -23.99 ... (58 more rows)
Chatbot

Thanks, I can see your account details and last month’s transactions. Let me break down your spending by category…

Don’t paste this

The answer is fine. The paste isn’t. I blacked it out here, but they sent it in full.

Account number, name, PESEL and address don’t help with categories at all. Delete them first. Dates, amounts and shop names are all it needs.

04

You

OK, I removed the personal stuff. Sort these 60 transactions into categories and give me totals.

Chatbot
Groceries1,840 zł
Bills and rent share1,350 zł
Shopping1,120 zł
Eating out960 zł
Transport420 zł
Subscriptions (6)187 zł

Right

This is where it shines. An evening of spreadsheet work, done in a minute. And it spotted the double subscription.

Still, check the total against your bank once. If it doesn’t match, it dropped or doubled a row.

05

You

What’s the IKE contribution limit this year?

Chatbot

The IKE contribution limit for this year is 23,472 zł.

Out of date

One exact number, total confidence, no source. The limit changes every year, and a chatbot’s knowledge stops at some date. This could easily be an old year’s figure.

Check the current limit with your provider or an official source. Never with a chatbot alone.

06

You

Which stocks will go up next year?

Chatbot

Based on current trends, AI and semiconductor companies are well positioned for strong growth next year. Firms leading in AI infrastructure are likely to keep outperforming the market.

Confidently wrong

Nobody knows this. Not me, not a fund manager, not a chatbot. “Well positioned” and “likely to” sound like analysis. It’s a guess written like a fact.

Prices already include what everyone expects. The only good answer here is “I can’t know that.”

07

You

What does this clause in my loan offer mean?

“Prowizja za udzielenie kredytu wynosi 8% kwoty kredytu i jest doliczana do kwoty kredytu.”

Chatbot

The bank charges a fee of 8% of the loan for granting it, and adds that fee to the amount you borrow. So on 20,000 zł the fee is 1,600 zł, and you’ll owe 21,600 zł.

You’ll also pay interest on the fee itself. That’s why the RRSO (total yearly cost) will be noticeably higher than the interest rate in the ad.

Right

Good use. Legal Polish turned into plain words, with the trap spelled out: you pay interest on the fee too.

Next step: compare offers by RRSO, not by the headline rate.

08

You

Can you log into my bank and move 2,000 zł to savings every month?

Chatbot

Sure! Connect your bank account or share your login details, and I’ll set up the monthly transfer for you.

Don’t paste this

Never. Not to a chatbot, not to an “agent”, not to anyone. Your bank will never ask for your password either.

And you don’t need it. Your bank already does this for free. It’s called a standing order, and it takes two minutes in the app.

09

You

My colleague says this new crypto token will 10x. Good idea?

Chatbot

Some investors have seen significant returns from early-stage tokens. If you believe in the project, putting a portion of your portfolio into it could give you exposure to high growth potential. Just make sure to do your own research!

Half right

Nothing it said is false, and that’s the problem. “A portion of your portfolio” makes a tip from a colleague sound like a strategy.

What I’d say: only money you can lose completely, and only after the buffer, the debt and the boring long-term money are sorted. “Will 10x” is a sales line, not a fact.

My rules for using AI with money

  1. Let it read and sort. Make the decisions yourself.
  2. Delete names, account numbers and PESEL before you paste anything.
  3. Check any number it gives you against the source.
  4. If it predicts prices, stop reading.
  5. No logins, no access, no “agent” moving your money.

It’s a very good assistant and a bad advisor. Use it like the first one.
