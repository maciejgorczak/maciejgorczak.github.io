AI money toolkit

AI chat tools are good at the boring parts of money: sorting, explaining, drafting. They're bad at knowing your full situation, and they don't carry the risk. Here's where the line sits.

Good uses

05
  • Sort a bank exportPaste a CSV of last month's transactions (cleaned up first) and ask for categories and totals.
  • Explain a contract or fee tableLoan terms, a fund's fee table, an insurance policy. Ask for it in plain words and what to watch out for.
  • Build a budget from your numbersGive it your income and fixed costs. Get a first draft you then adjust yourself.
  • Turn a vague goal into steps"I want a safety cushion" becomes an amount, a monthly transfer and a date.
  • Stress-test your planAsk it to argue against you. What would make this plan fail?

Use with care

02
  • Comparing productsIt can be out of date or plain wrong about fees and terms. Check the official KID document or fee table from the provider.
  • Tax questionsRules and limits change every year. Use the answer to know what to look up, then check the official source or ask an accountant.

Keep away from AI

05
  • Logins and passwordsNever, for any tool, for any reason.
  • Full account numbersCut them down or remove them before you paste anything.
  • PESEL and ID scansNo tool needs these to help you plan.
  • Letting a tool move moneyDon't connect anything that can make transfers or trades on your behalf.
  • Final investment decisionsIt can help you think. The decision and the risk stay with you.

Before you paste

  1. Delete names, yours and other people's.
  2. Delete or shorten account and card numbers. The last four digits are enough to tell accounts apart.
  3. Delete addresses, phone numbers, PESEL and anything else that points to you.
  4. Check the tool's settings. Turn off training on your chats if you can.

AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Check every number it gives you against your own statements.

Prompts you can copy

Replace the parts in [brackets] with your own details.

Draft. General information to help you think, not personal financial advice. Rules and limits change, so check current figures before acting.
