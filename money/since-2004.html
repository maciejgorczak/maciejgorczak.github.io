10,000 zł
since 2004

Say you put 10,000 zł in a drawer in 2004, or left it in a current account paying nothing. The number never changed. Here’s what happened to what it buys, using Polish consumer price data from GUS.

What it buys in 2024 5,229 zł

The 10,000 zł still says 10,000 zł. In 2004 prices, it buys about half of what it used to.

What you’d need in 2024 19,125 zł

To buy the same basket of things that 10,000 zł bought in 2004.

What 10,000 zł buys, in 2004 prices What you’d need to match 2004 10,000 zł

People call money in a current account “safe”. It’s safe from one thing: the number on the screen going down. It isn’t safe from prices going up. Between 2004 and 2024, average consumer prices in Poland almost doubled. The balance stayed at 10,000 zł. What it buys roughly halved.

Most years it’s slow. Two or three percent a year doesn’t feel like anything, and in 2014 to 2016 prices even dipped a little. Then 2022 and 2023 came, and prices rose about 27% in two years. That’s the part everyone remembers. But the quiet years before it added up to more: about 45% from 2004 to 2021.

This doesn’t mean everything should go into shares. A buffer for emergencies should sit somewhere boring and easy to reach. It means money you won’t need for years shouldn’t sit at 0%. Where it should sit depends on when you need it back.

Cash doesn’t lose value in one go. It loses it a couple of percent at a time, while nobody’s looking.

Source: GUS (Statistics Poland), annual average consumer price index, previous year = 100. Annual consumer price indices on stat.gov.pl.

Method: the base is the 2004 average price level. Each year’s price level is the previous one times that year’s index. “Buys” is 10,000 zł divided by the price level. “You’d need” is 10,000 zł times the price level. Annual averages smooth out what happened within each year. Your own basket may have risen faster or slower than the average.
