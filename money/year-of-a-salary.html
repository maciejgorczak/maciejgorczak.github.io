A year of one salary

One person. 8,000 zł a month after tax. A regular job and no interest in finance. This is what twelve months look like when they change a few boring things, in the order I’d change them.

Scroll · 9 months · each line is 200 zł

January

Payday. Then nothing.

8,000 zł lands on the 10th. Rent, bills and the car loan take 4,200. Day-to-day takes 3,000. The last 800 goes somewhere, and nobody could tell you where.

By the 9th of next month the account is close to zero again. Nothing is wrong, exactly. Nothing is being kept either.

February

Look before you change anything.

One evening, export three months of transactions as a CSV. Delete names and account numbers, then ask an AI to sort them into categories. Ten minutes. By hand, it’s the evening you never find.

The 800 turns out to be four things: subscriptions nobody uses (200), bank and card fees (100), small impulse orders (100) and food delivery on tired evenings (400).

March

Cut the easy ones. Watch what happens.

Cancel the subscriptions. Move to an account without fees. Delete saved cards from the shops you buy from on autopilot. Leaks drop from 800 to 400.

And the 400 you freed? Gone by the end of the month, spent on something else. Money that isn’t moved on purpose gets spent. That’s not a character flaw. It’s how a current account works.

April

Save first. Spend what’s left.

A standing order: 1,000 zł to a separate savings account, the day after payday. Day-to-day shrinks to 2,400. The first month feels a bit tight. By the second, nobody notices.

This is the most useful thing in the whole year. It took five minutes in the banking app.

May

The buffer starts to fill.

Two months in, there’s 2,000 zł in an account you don’t look at. The usual rule of thumb is a buffer of three to six months of essential costs.

Here, essentials are about 5,500 zł a month. So the first mark on the container is one month, the top is three. It’s slow. It’s supposed to be.

July

The car breaks. Nothing else does.

The buffer is at 3,000 by June, 4,000 after July’s transfer. Then the clutch goes: 2,800 zł.

Last year that would have gone on a credit card and stayed there for months. This year it comes out of the buffer, which drops to 1,200 and starts filling again. That’s the whole job of a buffer. It’s not an investment. It’s a shock absorber.

September

The money you forgot about.

You finally open the PPK app. It’s been running all year. On about 11,000 zł gross, 2% of your pay (220 zł) goes in before your net pay, and your employer adds 1.5% (165 zł).

That’s 385 zł a month, 3,465 so far, not counting returns or state top-ups. When you started the job you nearly opted out. That would have meant turning down the employer’s part.

November

A raise. Half of it goes away on purpose.

Net pay goes up by 400 zł. Before you get used to it, half goes to the standing order, which rises to 1,200. The other half makes daily life a bit easier.

You still feel the raise. You just don’t let all of it turn into a nicer version of the same month. The buffer is now close to one month of essentials.

December

The year, side by side.

Leaked: 5,600 zł, most of it in the first two months. Kept in the buffer: 6,600 zł, after paying for a clutch in cash. In PPK: 4,620 zł, of which 1,980 came from the employer.

If January had simply repeated twelve times, the leaks alone would have been 9,600 zł, the buffer would be empty and the clutch would still be on a card.

The raise came in November. It was the smallest part of the year.

Nothing here needed a higher salary, a finance hobby or a clever investment. What changed was the order: see where the money goes, stop the easy leaks, move money on payday, keep a buffer, take the employer money.

Investing comes next year, once the buffer reaches three months. That’s a different story.

  • An example person with rounded numbers. Each line in the drawing is 200 zł.
  • Essential costs are taken as 5,500 zł a month: fixed costs plus groceries. The buffer container is drawn to three months, 16,500 zł. All containers use the same scale.
  • PPK uses the basic rates (2% you, 1.5% employer) on an assumed 11,000 zł gross, kept flat after the raise. No interest, returns or state top-ups are counted.
